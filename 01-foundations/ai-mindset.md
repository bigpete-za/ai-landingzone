# AI Mindset

## The useful mental model

A generative AI assistant is a capable language-and-content tool that predicts and generates responses from patterns in data and the context it receives. It can be an excellent drafting partner, explainer, organiser, brainstorming partner and analyst of supplied material. It is not automatically a source of truth, a policy authority or an accountable decision-maker.

Think of it as a **fast, capable junior colleague who has not been briefed properly and may sound confident when wrong**. Give it a task, context, source material and constraints. Review its work.

## Strong use cases

- Starting a blank page: outlines, first drafts, alternative phrasings and examples.
- Reducing cognitive load: summarising long material, extracting actions and structuring notes.
- Exploring: brainstorming, comparing options, asking clarifying questions and identifying gaps.
- Transforming: changing tone, format, reading level, length or audience.
- Supporting analysis: classifying, comparing or finding patterns in supplied data, subject to checking.
- Learning: explaining unfamiliar concepts and creating practice exercises.

## Weak or higher-risk use cases without controls

- Unverified facts, citations, quotations, calculations or current information.
- Decisions affecting employment, credit, eligibility, health, safety, legal rights or finances.
- Sending messages, making commitments or changing records without review.
- Sensitive data in consumer or unapproved tools.
- Tasks where an incorrect answer would be costly and there is no independent way to verify it.

## Practical operating model

1. **Human frames the job:** goal, audience, constraints and acceptable risk.
2. **AI assists:** draft, analyse, organise, compare or explain using approved context.
3. **Human verifies:** accuracy, tone, completeness, source fidelity and consequences.
4. **Human owns the outcome:** approve, edit, communicate or act.

## Avoid two extremes

- **AI scepticism:** refusing to use it for low-risk, reversible work where it can save time.
- **AI delegation:** treating a plausible answer as proof, or handing over authority without controls.

Aim for appropriate use: start with small, reversible tasks; build confidence through review; increase autonomy only when the workflow and controls justify it.

## Exercise

Pick one recurring task. Record how long it usually takes, the inputs it needs, what a good result looks like and what could go wrong. Ask AI to help with one subtask, then compare the result with your normal process. Keep it only if quality is maintained and the overall effort improves.
