# Research Brief

## Use when

The user needs a sourced overview, options comparison or decision brief.

## Required inputs

- Research question and intended decision/audience.
- Geographic and time scope.
- Whether research is limited to supplied sources or may use public web sources.
- Preferred length and any required criteria.

## Procedure

1. Restate the question, scope and success criteria.
2. Identify ambiguities and ask for critical missing scope before research.
3. Prioritise primary sources, official documentation, original research and reputable independent analysis.
4. Record title, publisher, URL, date and the specific claim each source supports.
5. Compare claims and note disagreement, bias, missing evidence and outdated material.
6. Draft the brief with facts, analysis and assumptions separated.
7. Check that each citation resolves and supports the claim.
8. Provide a concise conclusion only to the extent supported by evidence.

## Output

- Executive summary
- Scope and method
- Findings with source links
- Comparison/options table if relevant
- Risks, caveats and disagreements
- Unknowns and follow-up questions
- Sources and access dates

## Failure behaviour

If no reliable source supports a claim, label it unverified. Do not invent citations, dates, statistics or direct quotations.

## Human approval

The user reviews sources and any decision recommendation before the brief is circulated or used.
