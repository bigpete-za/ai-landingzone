# SME AI Adoption Playbook

A small organisation does not need a large AI programme to start. It does need clear sponsorship, approved tools, sensible information handling and feedback from people doing the work.

## 1. Establish guardrails

- Name an executive sponsor and a practical business/IT owner.
- Publish approved tools, account rules and data-handling boundaries.
- Define what information must never be entered into general AI tools.
- Explain human accountability, disclosure expectations and incident reporting.
- Review permissions and oversharing in connected work repositories before enabling broad search or agents.

## 2. Select a focused pilot

Pick 3–5 frequent tasks that are low-risk, measurable and easy to review. Examples: meeting summaries, first drafts, email rewrites, internal knowledge summaries and report outlines. Avoid starting with autonomous decisions or high-impact employee/customer outcomes.

## 3. Enable people

- Demonstrate the approved tool using realistic, anonymised examples.
- Teach the prompt frame: Goal, Context, Source, Expectations.
- Practise verification and safe-use checks, not just prompt-writing.
- Create a small champion network and feedback channel.
- Share short examples people can adapt, not rigid scripts they must memorise.

## 4. Capture what works

For each shared prompt or skill, record its purpose, audience, tool, inputs, data class, owner, test results, limitations and review date. Remove duplicates and retire assets that are stale or ineffective.

## 5. Measure outcomes

Measure more than usage or claimed time savings:

- Cycle time before/after, including human review time.
- Rework and error rates.
- Quality and completeness.
- User confidence and adoption across roles.
- Customer or employee impact where measurable and appropriate.
- Data incidents, policy exceptions and near misses.

## 6. Improve deliberately

Review the pilot after a defined period. Keep workflows that improve outcomes safely. Redesign or stop workflows that introduce errors, hidden costs, privacy concerns or excessive review effort. Re-check product terms and features periodically.

## Example first-month sequence

| Period | Focus | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| Week 1 | Guardrails and baseline | Approved-tool/data note; 3–5 candidate tasks |
| Week 2 | Small pilot and training | Hands-on sessions; anonymised test cases |
| Week 3 | Library and feedback | First prompts/skills with owners and review dates |
| Week 4 | Evaluate and decide | Scorecard, lessons, next pilot or stop decisions |

This is an example cadence, not a compliance standard or a promise of results.
